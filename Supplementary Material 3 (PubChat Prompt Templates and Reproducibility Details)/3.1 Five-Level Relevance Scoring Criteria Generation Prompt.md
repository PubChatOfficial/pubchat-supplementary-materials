## [Key Reminders: How to Design High-Quality Scoring Criteria]

Based on the 12 examples above, please pay attention to the following key elements to ensure that the generated scoring criteria are detailed, explicit, and actionable:

### 1. Understand Concept Family Relationships

If the user query involves a broad category concept, such as "diuretics," "immunosuppressants," "perioperative interventions," "air pollution," or "PD-1 inhibitors," the scoring criteria should:
- **Explicitly list the main members of that family**, such as "diuretics include furosemide, torsemide, mannitol, spironolactone, etc."; apply this principle by analogy
- **Emphasize that studying any specific member is 3 points**
- Do not only say "studying diuretics = 3 points"; list the names of specific drugs, methods, or pollutants

### 2. Clearly Define Secondary Outcomes and Related Outcomes

If the user query involves a primary outcome, the scoring criteria should:
- **Clearly list which outcomes are secondary outcomes (2 points)**
- **Clearly list which outcomes are related outcomes (1 point)**
- **Emphasize that literature studying secondary outcomes is 2 points, not 0 points**
- Do not let the screening AI infer for itself what counts as a secondary outcome

**Example** (refer to Example 7; apply this principle by analogy):
- User query: "Evaluate the effect of prenatal or infant vitamin D supplementation for preventing childhood asthma"
- The scoring criteria should clearly state:
  - Primary outcomes mentioned in the abstract (3 points): asthma diagnosis, asthma incidence, persistent wheeze
  - Only secondary outcomes mentioned in the abstract (2 points): respiratory tract infections, wheezing episodes, atopic dermatitis
  - Only related outcomes mentioned in the abstract (1 point): bone health, growth and development, immune function

### 3. Flexible Understanding of Comparative Studies

If the user query involves evaluating the accuracy or effect of a tool or method, the scoring criteria should:
- **Clearly state that "comparative research is central to assessing value"**
- **Emphasize that studies comparing multiple tools or methods at the same time are still 3 points**, such as "simultaneously comparing HADS-A and GAD-7 is still 3 points"
- Do not downgrade a study for being "impure"

### 4. Research Methods and Data Matter More Than Conclusions

The scoring criteria should:
- **Emphasize attention to research methods and data, not only conclusions**
- **If a study reports relevant data, such as diagnostic accuracy data or efficacy data, it should still be included even if the main conclusion focuses on another aspect**

**Example** (refer to Example 4):
- User query: "Evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of molecular detection methods for neonatal sepsis"
- If a study's main conclusion concerns "bacterial load" or "pathogen distribution," but it reports diagnostic accuracy data such as sensitivity and specificity for PCR vs blood culture, it should still be 3 points
- This is because the study methods and data match the query's core interest, even if the conclusion has a different emphasis

### 5. Reasonable Extension of Populations and Settings

The scoring criteria should:
- **Reasonably extend definitions of populations and settings without being overly narrow**
- Example: "adults" should include adults aged 18 years and older, older adults, and specific populations such as cancer patients or pregnant women
- Example: "early screening" should include screening of asymptomatic populations and high-risk populations
- Example: "real-world study" should include retrospective cohorts, prospective cohorts, registry studies, database studies, and similar designs

### 6. Value of Subgroup Analyses and Specific Settings

If the user query involves a disease or population, the scoring criteria should:
- **Recognize the value of studies in specific subgroups or specific settings**
**Examples** (refer to Examples 8 and 10):
- User query: "Evaluate the effects of immunosuppressants for treating systemic lupus erythematosus"
  - A study of "lupus nephritis," a specific organ involvement, is also 3 points

  - A study of "older patients with type 2 diabetes" is also 3 points
